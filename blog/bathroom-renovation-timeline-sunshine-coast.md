# How Long Does a Bathroom Renovation Take? A Realistic Sunshine Coast Timeline

If you are planning a bathroom renovation on the Sunshine Coast, one of the first questions is usually simple: how long am I actually going to be without the bathroom?

For a straightforward full bathroom renovation, a realistic on-site build is often around 3 to 5 weeks once demolition starts. Smaller jobs can move faster. Bathrooms with layout changes, custom cabinetry, structural work, apartment access requirements or longer material lead times can take 5 to 8 weeks or more.

The important part is separating construction time from planning time. A bathroom may only be physically under construction for a month, but good projects usually have several weeks of measuring, quoting, selections, ordering and scheduling before anyone starts pulling tiles off the wall.

At QBM Projects, we would rather give homeowners a realistic program than promise a two-week transformation and spend the next month explaining delays.

## TL;DR

- A standard full bathroom renovation on the Sunshine Coast often takes around 3 to 5 weeks on site.
- Allow extra time before construction for quoting, design, selections, ordering and scheduling trades.
- Layout changes, custom cabinetry, stone, special-order fixtures, apartments and approvals can extend the timeline.
- Waterproofing, curing and trade sequencing should not be rushed.
- The fastest renovation is usually the one properly planned before demolition begins.

## How long does a bathroom renovation usually take?

For most homeowners, the useful answer is not a single number. It is a range based on the type of bathroom you are renovating.

- A small or fairly simple bathroom renovation usually takes around **2 to 4 weeks** on site.
- A standard full renovation often takes **3 to 5 weeks**.
- Major layout changes commonly take **4 to 6 weeks**.
- High-end bathrooms with custom cabinetry, stone or special-order items can take **5 to 8 weeks or more**.
- Apartment bathroom renovations often take **4 to 8 weeks** depending on body corporate rules, access, lift bookings and work-hour restrictions.

Those are construction periods, not the total time from the first phone call to handover. Before the job starts, there is usually a separate planning period for the site inspection, scope, quote, design decisions, product selections, ordering and trade scheduling. If you want an imported tap, handmade tile or custom vanity, the lead time on that one item can be longer than the bathroom build itself.

## What happens before the renovation starts?

The best bathroom timelines are usually won before the first day on site. Much of the work happens while the existing bathroom is still completely usable.

### 1. Initial conversation

We start with the basics: where the property is, what you want to change, whether the layout is staying similar and what you want the finished bathroom to feel like. A Buderim family bathroom may be about improving storage and a layout for kids; a Mooloolaba apartment may focus on getting as much use as possible from a smaller footprint; a Minyama or Noosa project may involve more custom finishes and longer product lead times.

### 2. Site inspection and measure

We inspect the room properly rather than quoting an entire renovation from a few phone photos. We look at existing plumbing locations, drainage, windows and doors, wall positions, floor levels, ventilation, access, existing damage, electrical requirements and whether anything structural appears to be involved.

### 3. Scope and quote

Once the job is understood, we can define what is actually being built. The scope should be clear enough that you understand what is included and what is not. A good quote is not just a total at the bottom of the page.

### 4. Layout and selections

Selections can include tiles, vanity, basin, tapware, shower fittings, bath, toilet, mirrors, lighting, cabinetry, handles, niches, accessories and paint colours. You do not need to have all of this worked out before you contact us, but ideally the major items are locked in before construction begins.

### 5. Ordering materials

Stock availability matters. A standard vanity in a Brisbane warehouse may arrive quickly; a custom vanity, specialty bath, imported tapware or made-to-measure shower screen has a different timeline. **Pro tip:** if there is one fixture or finish you absolutely have to have, choose it early.

## A realistic bathroom renovation timeline, stage by stage

Every bathroom is different, but this gives you a good idea of how a properly sequenced renovation usually comes together.

### Week 1: Demolition, plumbing and electrical rough-in

#### Demolition and strip-out

For a standard bathroom, demolition can take roughly one to three days depending on the existing construction. The old vanity, fixtures, tiles, shower screen and linings are removed as required so we can properly see what is underneath.

Older Sunshine Coast homes, particularly established properties around Buderim, Caloundra and parts of Maroochydore, can reveal water damage, rotten timber, previous waterproofing failures, old plumbing, uneven floors, poor previous repairs or termite damage. Finding one of these does not automatically mean disaster; it means dealing with the actual condition of the home rather than covering it back up.

#### Plumbing and electrical rough-in

If the layout is changing, plumbing and drainage are adjusted before walls and floors are closed back up. Moving a vanity a short distance is one thing; moving a toilet across the bathroom or creating a new walk-in shower can involve considerably more work. Lighting, exhaust fans, heated towel rails and power points are also roughed in while access is open.

### Week 2: Walls, floors and waterproofing

Any required wall framing, floor preparation, sheeting and substrate work is completed before waterproofing. This stage is less exciting to look at, but it matters enormously: large-format tiles will not hide a poorly prepared wall.

#### Waterproofing

This is one of the stages we will not rush. [QBCC guidance highlights waterproofing as a major defect area in residential construction][1] and stresses correct preparation, compliance with standards, manufacturer instructions, adequate curing time, inspections before work is covered, and proper records and certificates.

Trying to save time here is false economy. You might not see the membrane once the bathroom is finished, but it protects the structure behind thousands of dollars of tiles, cabinetry and fixtures.

### Week 3: Tiling and cabinetry preparation

Tiling is usually one of the longest visible stages of a bathroom renovation. Time depends on tile size and pattern, the number of walls tiled, niches, mitred edges, feature walls, floor falls, cutting and room shape. A good tiler is setting out the whole room so grout lines, corners, niches, fixtures and sight lines make sense once it is finished.

If the vanity is custom-made, it should already be well underway. Installation is coordinated around tiling and final plumbing fit-off. Stone benchtops can add another step if they need to be templated after cabinetry installation.

### Week 4: Fit-off, glass, painting and finishing

This is when the bathroom finally starts looking finished. Typical work includes:

- Vanity, basin and tapware installation
- Toilet and shower fittings
- Mirrors
- Lighting
- Exhaust fan
- Accessories
- Shower screen
- Painting
- Silicone and sealants
- Final adjustments
- Cleaning

The order matters. Good scheduling keeps trades out of each other's way and reduces the chance of finished work being damaged. Before handover, the room should be checked properly, including finishes, fixtures, operation and any required documentation for regulated work.

## When does a bathroom renovation take longer than four weeks?

Major layout changes, structural work, custom cabinetry, special-order fixtures, detailed tiling, hidden damage and apartment access can all extend the program. Lift bookings, loading areas, restricted work hours, body corporate rules and common-property protection can add time around the actual construction work.

---

## How apartment renovations affect the timeline

A bathroom renovation in a Mooloolaba, Maroochydore or Noosa apartment can be very different to renovating in a detached Buderim home. The bathroom may be similar in size, but the logistics are not. Apartment projects can involve:

- Body corporate approval
- Renovation applications
- Building-manager coordination
- Lift bookings
- Restricted work hours
- Common-area protection
- Waste removal rules
- Parking restrictions
- Noise rules
- Plumbing that affects common property

## Can you stay in the house during a bathroom renovation?

Usually, yes, if there is another usable bathroom in the home. If it is your only bathroom, consider:

- Shower and toilet access
- Dust and noise
- Children and pets
- Work-from-home arrangements
- Trade access during the day

If the only bathroom is being completely stripped out, some homeowners choose to stay elsewhere for part of the build.

## What causes bathroom renovation delays?

Most delays fall into a few categories:

- Late selections
- Materials not being on site
- Hidden conditions
- Variations during construction
- Custom fabrication
- Trade sequencing
- Approvals or body corporate requirements that were not sorted before scheduling

## How to keep your renovation on schedule

1. **Finalise the layout early.** Choose major products before demolition, particularly the vanity, tiles, bath, toilet and tapware.
2. **Order custom items early.** Lead times can be longer than you expect.
3. **Make decisions quickly** during the build if something unexpected comes up.
4. **Use one team** to manage the project, and allow a sensible contingency for genuine surprises.

---

## Do approvals add time to a bathroom renovation?

They can, depending on the scope. Straightforward internal bathroom work may be handled differently from renovations involving structural alterations, external walls, windows or more complex plumbing and drainage work. Confirm what applies to the actual scope before starting, especially where there are major structural changes or significant plumbing relocation.

---

## Why waterproofing should never be rushed

Bathrooms fail from the parts you cannot see just as often as the parts you can. [QBCC has identified waterproofing as a significant defect area][1] and warns builders and trades to focus on correct preparation, standards, manufacturer instructions, curing times, inspections and documentation. We would rather hand the bathroom over slightly later than hide a problem behind a beautiful tile.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a bathroom renovation really be done in two weeks?

A very simple bathroom may be completed quickly under the right circumstances, but two weeks is not a sensible blanket promise for a full strip-out and rebuild. For most complete renovations, allowing around 3 to 5 weeks on site is a more realistic starting point.

### How long should I allow before the renovation starts?

Allow time for the site inspection, quote, layout, selections, ordering and scheduling. Custom cabinetry, special-order fixtures or apartment approvals can push the pre-construction period out further.

### What is the longest part of a bathroom renovation?

Tiling is often one of the longest visible stages, particularly with floor-to-ceiling tiles, niches, mitres or large-format products. Waterproofing also needs the correct preparation and curing time before tiling.

### Does moving plumbing add much time?

It can. Moving a vanity slightly is very different to relocating a toilet or creating new drainage positions. The existing plumbing, floor structure and access all affect what is involved.

### Do apartment bathroom renovations take longer?

They can. Body corporate approvals, lift bookings, restricted work hours, common-area protection and waste removal rules can add time around the project.

### What happens if you find water damage after demolition?

We stop, assess it and talk you through what needs to happen before covering it back up. Hidden damage can affect the timeline, but dealing with it properly is better than building a new bathroom over an old problem.
